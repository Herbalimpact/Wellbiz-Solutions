BRELA Registration: Common Mistakes That Delay Your Company Registration

Twelve avoidable mistakes that stall BRELA company registration in Tanzania — and exactly what to do instead.

🇹🇿 Soma kwa Kiswahili

If you have ever sat refreshing your ORS account every morning, hoping to see that magic word "Approved," you already know the feeling this article is about. Registering a company with BRELA should be a straightforward, exciting step in building your business, yet so many entrepreneurs in Tanzania find themselves stuck for weeks, sometimes months, chasing a certificate of incorporation that should have taken a matter of days. The good news is that almost every delay in BRELA company registration traces back to a small, avoidable mistake. Once you know what to look out for, you can move through the BRELA Online Registration System, commonly called ORS, with far less stress and far fewer surprises.

Let us walk through the mistakes that trip people up most often, and what you can do instead.

1. Getting the company name wrong from the start

A lot of registration headaches begin right here, at the name reservation stage. People often submit only one preferred name, and when it turns out to be too close to an existing registered company or trademark, the whole process stalls while they think of alternatives. Others choose names that are too generic or purely descriptive, which BRELA is likely to reject outright. Save yourself the back and forth by preparing two or three well thought out name options before you even log in, and check that none of them closely resemble a business already on the register. You can check this yourself with a name search — it's a quick digital search within the same registration system.

2. Letting small name and ID mismatches slip through

This one catches even careful business owners off guard. The name you type into the ORS form must match your national ID or passport exactly, down to the last middle name or initial. If your NIDA card reads "John Peter Mwakasege" and you type "John P. Mwakasege" into the system, that tiny difference can be enough to trigger a rejection or a request for correction, pushing your timeline back by days. Before you submit anything, sit your ID document next to your screen and type every name precisely as it appears.

3. Leaving out director and shareholder details, or getting them incomplete

Every director and shareholder needs their National Identification Number from NIDA, and local directors also need a Tax Identification Number. Foreign directors and shareholders need valid passport details instead. A surprising number of applications get held up simply because someone forgot to gather these numbers in advance, or because a director's details were entered in a hurry and something does not match official records. Remember too that most company types need a minimum of two directors, so confirm early who those people will be and gather their documents before you start the online form, not while you are halfway through it.

4. Giving a registered office address that cannot be verified

Your registered office is not just a formality. It is the address where official notices are sent, and BRELA wants it to be real and locatable, not a vague description or a post office box standing in for a physical location. Applications sometimes stall when the address given is incomplete or when it does not match supporting documents like a tenancy agreement. Take a few minutes to write out your plot number, street, ward, and any nearby landmark clearly, exactly as you would if you were guiding someone to find you in person.

5. Forgetting the Beneficial Ownership declaration

This is one of the newer requirements that catches people out because it feels like an extra, easily missed step. Tanzanian law now requires companies to submit details of their beneficial owners, meaning the real individuals who ultimately own or control the company, separately from the usual shareholder information. Skipping this, or assuming it is covered elsewhere in the form, is a common reason applications sit unprocessed. Treat it as its own checklist item rather than an afterthought.

6. Letting your ORS session expire before you finish

Here is something many first time applicants do not realise: once you start a new company registration on ORS, you typically have only a handful of days, often around five, to complete and submit it. If life gets in the way and you leave the form half filled for too long, you may find yourself starting over from scratch.

Wellbiz tip: Before you begin, gather every document and detail you will need — directors' information, the memorandum and articles of association, your registered office details, and your business activity classification — so you can move through the form in one focused sitting rather than in scattered attempts.

7. Signing, dating, or uploading documents incorrectly

Once your information is entered, ORS generates a consolidated form and an integrity form that need to be downloaded, printed, signed, dated, and sometimes stamped, before being scanned back into the system along with your memorandum and articles of association. A shockingly common cause of delay is a form that was signed but not dated, stamped in the wrong place, or scanned so poorly that an officer cannot read it clearly. Use a proper scanner or a good scanning app rather than a rushed phone photo, and double check every signature and date before you upload.

8. Choosing the wrong business activity classification

BRELA asks you to describe your company's objectives according to the ISIC classification system, a standard list of economic activities. Many applicants either pick a classification that does not really match what their business does, or leave it too vague, and this can lead to queries from the registrar that add days to the process. Take a moment to think through exactly what your company will be doing day to day, then choose the classification that reflects that reality as closely as possible.

9. Missing supporting statutory forms like Form 14b

Alongside your memorandum and articles of association, certain forms, such as the declaration of compliance, need to be properly completed and, in many cases, signed by an advocate or notary public. Applicants who are unfamiliar with these requirements sometimes submit their main documents while forgetting these supporting forms entirely, only to be sent back to fix the gap. It helps enormously to work from a full checklist of required documents rather than relying on memory.

10. Assuming registration is the finish line and missing annual returns

Getting your certificate of incorporation feels like crossing the finish line, but it is really just the start of your compliance journey. Companies are required to file annual returns within a set window, often around twenty eight days after the anniversary of incorporation, and missing this deadline brings penalties and marks your company as non-compliant on the register. This will not delay your first registration, but it absolutely delays and complicates everything that comes afterward, so mark that date on your calendar the moment your certificate arrives.

11. Forgetting that some businesses need extra licenses beyond BRELA

BRELA registration gives you legal existence as a company, but depending on what you do, you may also need sector specific licenses or permits, from local government business licenses to industry regulator approvals. Entrepreneurs sometimes discover this requirement late, after they thought registration was complete, and have to pause operations while they sort it out. Research what your specific sector requires before you register, so there are no unwelcome surprises afterward.

12. Not following up when things seem to be taking too long

Finally, one of the simplest but most overlooked steps is following up. Processing usually takes anywhere from about seven to fourteen working days depending on how busy BRELA is, but if your application seems to be stuck well beyond that, do not just wait quietly. Use the track application feature on ORS, or reach out to BRELA support directly, to find out what is happening. Often a quick query can be resolved in a single conversation, saving you weeks of uncertainty.

Company registration with BRELA does not have to be the stressful, drawn out process so many people describe. Most delays come down to small details — a mismatched name here, a missing signature there, an overlooked declaration somewhere else — and every single one of them is entirely preventable once you know to look for it. Take your time gathering documents before you start, double check every entry against your official records, and follow up promptly if things stall. Do that, and there is a very good chance you will be holding your certificate of incorporation sooner than you expect.
