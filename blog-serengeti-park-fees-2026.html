Serengeti National Park Fees 2026/27: What You'll Really Pay

🇹🇿 Soma kwa Kiswahili

When people get their first safari quote for Tanzania, the same question comes up: "Why is it so expensive?" A big part of the answer is park fees. In the Serengeti, they are often the largest single cost after accommodation, and they are the same whether you travel on a budget or in luxury.

The good news: once you understand how the fees work, you can plan smarter and avoid paying more than you need to. Here's everything in plain language.

Figures include 18% VAT and are the rates most widely reported for 2026. Some sources list lower low-season rates. Fees can change at short notice, so always confirm with TANAPA or your operator.

1. The conservation fee is charged per 24 hours, not per night

This is the rule that surprises most visitors. The clock starts when you pass through the gate. If you enter at 11 am on Monday, your first 24-hour period ends at 11 am on Tuesday. Stay one minute longer and you pay another full day.

So a "2-night" stay can cost 2 or 3 days of fees, depending on when you enter and leave. A good operator plans your entry and exit times to avoid an extra day.

2. Sleeping inside the park adds a concession fee

Lodges and tented camps inside the Serengeti pay a concession fee for every guest, every night, and pass it on to you. It's about $70.80 per adult per night. It's usually included in your safari quote or added to your lodge bill, so check which.

Is it worth it? Often, yes. Staying inside means you're already among the animals at sunrise and sunset, when they're most active, instead of spending hours driving in and out.

3. Most routes also pass through Ngorongoro

If you drive to the Serengeti from Arusha, you pass through the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, which has its own fees, reported at about $70.80 per adult per 24 hours. You pay it even if you're only driving through.

Going down into the Ngorongoro Crater itself adds a crater service fee of $295 per vehicle per descent. It's per vehicle, not per person, so it's cheaper per head when you share.

5. How you pay

  • Park gates are cashless. Payment is by Visa or Mastercard.
  • Most visitors never pay at the gate, because their safari operator prepays the fees and includes them in the quote.
  • Ask your operator to show park fees as a separate line. If they won't, ask why.

6 ways to pay less without missing out

  1. Plan entry and exit times so you don't pay for an extra 24-hour period.
  2. Stay just outside the park on some nights to avoid the concession fee.
  3. Spend more time in cheaper parks. Tarangire and Lake Manyara cost about $53–$59 a day, and the southern parks less.
  4. Share a vehicle. The crater fee and vehicle costs are split between more people.
  5. Travel in the green season (November to early December, or March to May) for lower lodge prices.
  6. Compare written quotes for exactly the same itinerary, with park fees shown separately.

Don't forget the costs outside the park

Park fees are only part of the picture. In 2026, also budget for your visa ($50 for most nationalities, $100 for US citizens) and the new mandatory travel insurance for mainland Tanzania ($44 per person, introduced on 1 October 2026). Zanzibar has its own $44 policy, and the rules on whether one policy covers both were still being clarified in October 2026, so check before you fly.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Serengeti entrance fee in 2026?

Most 2026 sources report $82.60 per non-resident adult (16+) per 24 hours, including VAT, and $23.60 for children aged 5–15. Children under 5 enter free.

Is the fee charged per day or per night?

Per 24 hours, counted from the time you enter the park.

What is the concession fee?

An extra fee of about $70.80 per adult per night for staying in a lodge or camp inside the park. It's usually included in your quote or lodge bill.

Can I pay park fees in cash?

No. The gates are cashless, so pay by Visa or Mastercard, or let your operator prepay.
