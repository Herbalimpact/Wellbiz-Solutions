Business Name vs Limited Company in Tanzania: Which One Should You Register?

A clear look at cost, personal liability, and growth potential — so you can choose your registration path with confidence, not guesswork.

🇹🇿 Soma kwa Kiswahili

Almost every entrepreneur in Tanzania reaches this same fork in the road early on. You have your idea, your first customers are lining up, and now BRELA is asking you to choose a registration path. Do you register a simple business name, or do you go the fuller route of a limited company? The two options sound similar on the surface, but they lead to very different journeys in terms of cost, protection, and how far your business can eventually grow. Let us walk through the real differences so you can choose with confidence rather than guesswork.

1. What a business name registration actually is

A business name registration, sometimes called a trade name, is the simplest way to formalise a business run by one person or a small partnership. It is registered under the Business Names Act through BRELA, and it essentially tells the public who is behind a particular trading name. What it does not do is create a separate legal entity. In the eyes of the law, you and your business remain the same person, which keeps things simple but comes with a catch we will get to shortly.

2. What a limited company actually is

A limited company, most commonly registered as a Private Limited Company, is a different kind of creature altogether. Once BRELA issues your certificate of incorporation, your company becomes its own legal person, separate from you. It can own property, sign contracts, borrow money, and even be sued, all in its own name. You typically need at least two directors and two shareholders to set one up, along with a Memorandum and Articles of Association that lay out how the company will be run.

3. How the cost and speed compare

If budget and speed are your biggest concerns right now, a business name wins easily. Registration fees sit in the tens of thousands of shillings, with a small annual maintenance fee, and the whole process can be completed in under a week. A limited company costs considerably more once you add up name reservation, incorporation filing based on your share capital, stamp duty, and document filing fees, and the process itself typically takes a few weeks rather than days. Neither price tag is out of reach, but they do call for different levels of upfront planning.

4. Where personal liability makes all the difference

This is the point that matters most once your business starts taking on real risk. With a business name, there is no legal wall between you and your business. If the business runs into debt or gets sued, your personal savings, car, or even your house can be on the line. With a limited company, your liability is generally limited to what you invested in shares. If the company fails, creditors cannot typically chase your personal assets beyond that investment. For anyone taking on loans, signing supplier contracts, or working in a higher risk sector, this protection alone can be worth the extra registration cost.

Wellbiz tip: If you're already signing supplier contracts, taking on business loans, or operating in construction, logistics, or manufacturing, the liability protection of a limited company often justifies the extra cost from day one — even if a business name looks like the cheaper starting point on paper.

5. What each option means for raising money and credit

Banks, investors, and even some larger clients tend to feel more comfortable dealing with a limited company. It has a clearer ownership structure through shares, it can bring in new investors without disrupting your personal finances, and its financial records are easier to separate from your household budget. A business name can still get you a loan or a supplier credit line, but you will often find the options narrower and the terms less generous, since the lender is really assessing you personally rather than a distinct business entity.

6. Whether you can change your mind later

Many entrepreneurs start with a business name because it feels safer while they are testing an idea, then convert to a limited company once things pick up. This is completely normal and BRELA's system is built to accommodate it. You are not locking yourself into one path forever. The more common regret runs the other way, business owners who scaled up quickly under a business name and later realised they needed a company structure sooner, once contracts, staff numbers, or borrowing needs grew faster than expected.

7. Which one bigger contracts and investment registration require

If you are eyeing government tenders, corporate supply contracts, or investment registration through the Tanzania Investment Centre, you will almost always need a limited company rather than a business name. Procurement rules and investment incentive schemes are generally written around companies because of the accountability and transparency that separate legal personality provides. If bigger contracts are part of your five year plan, it is worth registering as a company sooner rather than later, even if it costs a little more today.

8. What foreign owners and diaspora entrepreneurs need to know

If you are not a Tanzanian citizen, this decision is largely made for you. Business name registration is designed for citizens and residents operating small, individually owned trades, and recent regulations have further restricted non-citizens from a list of specific business activities altogether. Foreign investors, in almost every case, need to register a limited company to operate legally and to access any investment protections or incentives available to them.

9. A simple way to decide which is right for you

If you are testing an idea with low risk, working alone or with one partner, and want to start trading within days on a tight budget, a business name is a sensible starting point. If you already know you will be taking on debt, bringing in co-founders or investors, chasing tenders, or operating in a higher liability sector like construction, logistics, or manufacturing, go straight for a limited company. When in doubt, ask yourself one question, if this business failed tomorrow, how much of my personal life could it drag down with it. The answer usually points you to the right structure.

10. What happens after you register, whichever path you choose

Registration is only the beginning. A business name still needs an annual maintenance fee and a Taxpayer Identification Number for tax purposes. A limited company carries heavier ongoing duties, including annual returns filed with BRELA, proper company records, and closer attention to any changes in directors or shareholding. Whichever path fits you today, plan for these ongoing responsibilities from day one so nothing catches you off guard later.

There is no single right answer here, only the answer that fits where your business is now and where you intend to take it. A business name gets you moving quickly and cheaply. A limited company builds you a stronger foundation for growth, borrowing, and bigger opportunities, at a higher upfront cost and with more ongoing compliance. Take an honest look at your risk, your funding needs, and your five year ambitions, and the right choice tends to become clear.
