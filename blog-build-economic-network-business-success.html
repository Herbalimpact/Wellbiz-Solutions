Build an Economic Network That Will Carry You in 10 Years: Why Your Business Network Matters More Than You Think

We take networks for granted until we need them. Family and friends give support; an economic network of mentors, investors, financiers, distributors and suppliers is what makes a business grow.

🇹🇿 Soma kwa Kiswahili

There are things in this world we take lightly, but the truth is that their value is far greater than just being “fine”. Have you ever asked yourself what life would be like if you were completely alone in this world: no father, no mother, no brothers or sisters, no relatives on your father’s or your mother’s side?

The honest answer is that you would be terribly lonely. Today we take our families for granted, but their importance shows the moment they are not there. So what is a family? A family is a network of people bound together by blood. And once you see family as a network, a bigger question follows: what kind of business network are you building for the next ten years?

The Family Network: Support You Cannot Buy

The family network matters because it gives a kind of support that is hard to find anywhere else. If you fall ill and cannot do anything for yourself, it is usually your own children, and to some extent your brothers and sisters, who will clean up after you with a smile on their faces.

Friends and Acquaintances: Filling the Gaps

Beyond family there are other networks that matter too, such as the network of friends and acquaintances. This network often fills the gaps when you are far from your family, or when your family network is weak.

The Business Network: the One That Makes You Grow

Another very important network is the one made up of partners in economic activity. If you run a business, for example, it is very hard to make big economic progress without being part of this kind of network. It includes five groups of people:

WhoWhat they bringWhere to find them
Mentors and advisers (a mastermind group)Knowledge, experience, honest feedbackExperienced business owners, trade associations, seminars
InvestorsSeed money for a new idea, or growth capitalSuccessful business people, partners, angel investors
FinanciersLoans and working capitalBanks, microfinance institutions, SACCOS, savings groups
DistributorsReach: they take your product to more customersWholesalers, shops, agents, online sellers
SuppliersStock or raw materials, sometimes on creditManufacturers, importers, farmers, wholesale markets

Unlike the other networks, an economic network takes real effort and knowledge to build. Nobody is born into it. To build a strong one you have to invest in learning, so that you gain the knowledge it requires.

The Knowledge You Need Is Not Taught in School

It is important to understand that this kind of knowledge is usually not part of the regular school or university curriculum. For that reason, it helps to forget for a while that you have a degree, a doctorate or even a professorship, and to become a learner again.

This knowledge is found in books, seminars, lectures and conferences, especially books written by people who gathered a great deal of wisdom by doing, making mistakes, correcting them and finally getting it right. You can also learn directly from people who already have the knowledge. That is why the group of knowledgeable people should be the very first you welcome into your network.

What the evidence says: the U.S. Small Business Administration reports survey findings that about 70% of small businesses that receive mentoring survive five years or more, roughly double the rate of businesses without a mentor.

Investors: the Group That Plants the Seed

Investors are also very important in your network. They are the first to put seed money into your business idea (if it is a new business) or to help it grow (if it is already running). Very often the businesses we dream up never start, or never grow, simply because this group is missing.

The surprising thing is that people who are already very rich are the ones who best understand the power of networks, and they invest heavily in learning how to build and use them. The people who need economic networks most, those who are struggling, often do not do the same. That is exactly what needs to change.

How to Build Your Business Network: 7 Practical Steps

  1. Map what you have. Write down the people you already know in each of the five groups. The gaps show you where to start.
  2. Find one mentor first. Choose someone a few steps ahead of you in your field. Ask specific questions and respect their time.
  3. Join where business people meet. A trade association, a chamber of commerce, a savings group or SACCOS, trade fairs and seminars.
  4. Treat suppliers and distributors as partners. Pay on time, keep your word and talk regularly. Reliable partners get better prices and credit.
  5. Give before you ask. Share useful information, refer customers, introduce people to each other. A network is a two-way road.
  6. Get investor-ready. Keep simple records and a clear business plan, so that when an investor or lender appears you can show them the numbers.
  7. Keep in touch. Spend one hour a week calling, visiting or messaging people in your network, not only when you need something.

A worked example: two tailors, ten years apart

Two tailors open small shops in the same year. The first works alone: buys fabric wherever it is cheapest that day, takes orders only from walk-in customers and borrows from relatives when money runs short. The second joins a traders’ association, finds an older tailor who becomes her mentor, buys fabric from two regular suppliers who now give her 30 days’ credit, supplies school uniforms through a shop owner she met at a trade fair, and keeps records good enough to qualify for a bank loan. Ten years later, the first is still cutting cloth alone; the second employs eight people. Same skill, different network. (An illustrative example.)

⚠️ An economic network is not network marketing. Real business relationships help both sides earn more through advice, capital, supply and sales. Be careful with any scheme that asks you to pay to join or where income depends mainly on recruiting other people.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an economic or business network?

It is the group of people who help you create, grow and protect income: mentors and advisers (a mastermind group), investors, financiers such as banks and savings groups, distributors who take your product to customers, and suppliers who give you stock or raw materials. Unlike family, you have to build it on purpose.

Who should I bring into my network first?

Start with people who have knowledge: mentors, experienced business owners and advisers. Their guidance helps you avoid costly mistakes and opens the door to the others. Studies of mentoring suggest mentored small businesses are far more likely to survive five years than those without a mentor.

How do I build a business network if I don't know anyone important?

Start where you are. Join a trade association, chamber of commerce or savings group, attend seminars and trade fairs, keep in touch with good suppliers and customers, and always offer value first: share information, refer customers, keep your promises. Trust grows from small, repeated actions.

Is building an economic network the same as network marketing?

No. An economic network is a set of genuine business relationships that help each side earn more: advice, capital, supply and sales. It does not involve recruiting people or paying to join. Be wary of any scheme where income depends mainly on signing up others.
