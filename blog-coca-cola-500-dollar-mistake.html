The $500 Mistake Hidden in Coca-Cola's History

The world's most recognizable soft drink almost never became a business empire at all. Coca-Cola was invented in 1886 in Atlanta, Georgia, by a pharmacist named Dr. John Pemberton. It went on sale for the first time on May 8, 1886, at Jacobs' Pharmacy, priced at just 5 cents a glass. The name "Coca-Cola" itself wasn't even Pemberton's idea — it came from his business partner, Frank M. Robinson.

A Slow, Uncertain Start

In its first year, Coca-Cola sold an average of just nine glasses a day. Dr. Pemberton never grasped the commercial potential of what he'd created. Bit by bit, he sold off shares in the business, and by 1888 — shortly before his death — he sold what remained of his stake to a man named Asa G. Candler for just $500.

From $500 to a Global Empire

Candler gradually bought out the other shareholders until he owned the entire company outright. As of 138 years after that $500 sale, Coca-Cola is valued at roughly $375 billion — meaning the company's value has grown by an average of about $2.7 billion a year, every year since 1888.

Why the Same Product Produced Two Different Fortunes

What separated Asa Candler's success from John Pemberton's struggle? Without question, it came down to each man's ability to sell. Candler was a master at getting people to buy — Pemberton, for all his skill as a chemist, had very little talent in that area.

A Story That Repeats Across Every Industry

The story of these two men repeats itself daily, at every level of business, all over the world. More often than not, the people who invent a product or service are not the ones who profit most from it. The people who profit most are almost always the ones skilled enough to sell it.

Selling Is What Actually Builds Wealth

Every company's economic success comes down to its ability to sell what it makes. That's exactly why the largest share of most companies' budgets goes to sales and marketing teams, not to the people who build the product. The lesson behind that spending is simple: produce without selling, and you'll eventually go broke.

Invest in Learning How to Sell

If you're already in business — or planning to start one — take this lesson seriously. Invest in learning how to sell, and everything else tends to fall into place. Selling is a skill, and like every skill, it comes at a cost. You have to be deliberate about spending a little to learn how to sell well, so your business can grow and reach the goals you've set for it.
